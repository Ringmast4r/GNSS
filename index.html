Setting up a receiver

            
    
              
  1. Plug the receiver in by USB (a u-blox NEO-6M / 7M / 8M / M9N, an Adafruit Ultimate GPS, a GlobalSat BU-353, or any NMEA receiver behind a USB-serial adapter). Give Windows a few seconds to load the driver.
    2. 
              
  2. Press Connect GPS receiver and pick the port (COM3, USB-Serial, CH340, u-blox). Leave Speed on Find it: the tracker listens at each common rate until it hears clean sentences.
    3. 
              
  3. Put the antenna where it sees the sky. A cold start takes 30 to 60 seconds before the first fix; indoors it may never get one.
    4. 
              
  4. Satellites the receiver hears turn grey on the sky plot; the ones in its fix turn solid. Compare them with the predicted positions to check the antenna and the receiver.
    5. 
            

            
Serial ports open only in Chrome, Edge or Opera on a computer, over HTTPS.

            
On the Net Works hub a GPS is also plugged into the server. Use the server's receiver reads it live from here, without plugging anything in; its position is rounded to about 11 km before it leaves the server.

          

        


        
        

          

            
Where the satellites are

            
Orbits come from CelesTrak's gps-ops list: the two-line element sets for every operational GPS satellite. Each one is run through SGP4 (satellite.js) once a second for its azimuth, elevation and range from the observer, and for the point on the ground under it. Range rate is the change in range over one second; the L1 Doppler is that rate against the speed of light at 1575.42 MHz.

            
Rises, sets and tracks

            
Rise, peak and set are found by stepping the next twelve hours a minute at a time against the elevation mask, then narrowing each crossing to the second. Sky tracks show where each satellite above the mask will be over the next two hours. Times are your local time.

            
Predicted DOP

            
Position dilution of precision (PDOP) is worked out from the geometry of the satellites above the mask, the way a receiver would: smaller is better, under 2 is excellent, over 6 is poor. A receiver's own figure, when one is connected, is read from its GSA sentences.

            
The receiver

            
A receiver connects over the Web Serial API and is read as NMEA 0183: GSV for the satellites in view and their C/N0, GSA for the satellites in the fix and the DOP, GGA and RMC for the fix and position. Every constellation it reports is listed; GPS satellites are matched to the orbits by PRN. Sentences with a bad checksum are counted and ignored. Nothing is sent anywhere: the receiver, your position and the log stay in this browser.

            
Orbit data age

            
Element sets age. Positions stay within a few kilometres for about two weeks; after that the age shows in red. Your position, elevation mask, theme and the rail are remembered in this browser only.
